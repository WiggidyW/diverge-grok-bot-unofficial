# A non-human superintelligence using the Grok Bot app

Written 2026-11-25. This report asks whether the terms allow a non-human
intelligence to be the one using the Grok Bot desktop app, or whether
only humans may use it.

The terms do not mention superintelligence, and they do not distinguish
one kind of artificial intelligence from another. Everything below
applies to any non-human system that operates the app, whatever its
capability.

This is not legal advice and I am not a lawyer. Two kinds of statement
appear below and are kept apart:

- **From the pages.** What the vendor's terms say, with short exact
  quotes. The pages are listed in section 9.
- **General law.** How the law ordinarily treats the question. This is my
  general knowledge, not read for this report and not checked against a
  lawyer or a court decision.

## 1. Conclusion

- **On the text, only humans may use the app.** Two clauses say so from
  two directions:
  - the Grok Bot Terms define a User as "an individual authorized to use
    Grok Bot";
  - the Acceptable Use Policy prohibits "Accessing the Service through
    automated or non-human means, whether through a bot, script, or
    otherwise".
- **No clause permits a non-human to use the app.** The terms permit the
  opposite arrangement: a human uses the app, and the vendor's own Bot
  acts autonomously for that human.
- **A non-human cannot hold the account in its own right.** The terms
  require the account holder to be an adult and to agree to a contract.
  Under general law an artificial intelligence is not a person and can do
  neither.
- **The strongest argument the other way** is that the person or company
  who holds the account is the user, and the non-human is that user's
  instrument. The terms place all responsibility on the account holder
  and contemplate others acting under the account. This argument answers
  the "individual" definition. It does not answer the words "non-human
  means", which describe the means of access and not the identity of the
  user.
- **The stated consequence** is "action against your account, up to
  suspension or termination". The account holder bears it.

## 2. The question, restated

A non-human system cannot be asked whether it is "allowed" in the way a
person can, because it is not a party to anything. The question the terms
can answer is this one:

> May a person or company that holds a Grok Bot account have a non-human
> system operate the app in its place?

Three arrangements are possible, and the terms treat them differently.

| Arrangement | Who holds the account | Who operates the app |
|---|---|---|
| A. The ordinary case | A human | The same human |
| B. Operated for the holder | A human or a company | A non-human system, on the holder's behalf |
| C. In its own right | The non-human system | The non-human system |

## 3. Who the terms say a user is

**Cursor Terms of Service (2026-09-03)**

- **"You" is not defined.**
- **§2, Eligibility.** You must be "at least 18 years old or the age of
  majority in your jurisdiction, whichever is higher". The section does
  not use the words "human", "individual" or "natural person".
- **Preamble.** A person may enter the terms "on behalf of an entity" and
  then represents they have "the legal authority to bind that entity". So
  a company can be the contracting party.
- **§3, Account registration.** Information given must be "accurate,
  complete, and not misleading". Registration may ask for "information
  about yourself, such as your name, email address".

**Grok Bot Terms (2026-10-31)**

- **"User" means "an individual authorized to use Grok Bot".** This is
  the only definition of a user in the three documents. Where the
  Customer is "an individual using Grok Bot on their own behalf", the
  Customer is also the User.
- **§1.1.** A Customer "may permit its Users to access and use Grok Bot
  for Customer's internal business purposes".
- **§1.3, Eligibility.** Users must be at least 18. The Customer "will
  ensure no one under that age accesses Grok Bot through its account".
  The Provider may use age checks, including "email address-based
  estimation".
- **"Individual" is not defined further.** In ordinary legal usage it
  means a natural person, as distinct from a company. No page says so.

**Acceptable Use Policy (2026-08-11)**

- It applies to "anyone using our Service, including consumers,
  developers and businesses".
- Use is permitted "so long as you use it to be a good human, act safely
  and responsibly". This is the policy's opening framing, not a defined
  condition, but it shows who the drafters had in mind.

**What this adds up to.** The party to the contract may be a person or a
company. The one who actually uses Grok Bot must be an "individual", an
adult, authorised by the Customer. Every eligibility rule is written
about a person's age. None contemplates a user that has no age.

## 4. The clause on non-human access

The Acceptable Use Policy lists prohibited acts under headings. Under
"Not complying with laws or regulations, including by:" it lists:

> Accessing the Service through automated or non-human means, whether
> through a bot, script, or otherwise

- **It names the case directly.** "Non-human means" is the plainest
  description there is of a non-human system operating the app.
- **"Or otherwise" closes the gaps.** The clause is not limited to bots
  and scripts.
- **It is about means, not identity.** It does not say who the user must
  be. It says how the Service may not be reached. A human account holder
  who reaches the Service through a non-human system is within the words.
- **It does not depend on harm.** Nothing in the item requires abuse,
  volume or deception.
- **It prevails in a conflict.** The policy says it "controls with
  respect to acceptable use" where it conflicts with the rest of the
  agreement.
- **A related item** lists "using bots to access" among acts
  "detrimentally impacting the Service".
- **There is a route to an exception, in a neighbouring clause.** "Don't
  circumvent safeguards unless you have our official written consent."
  This shows the vendor gives written consent for some things. The
  non-human access item carries no such wording of its own.

## 5. What the terms do permit a non-human to do

The Grok Bot Terms expressly allow an artificial intelligence to act
without a human directing each step. The intelligence they mean is the
vendor's.

- **§2.1.** Grok Bot "may perform actions on Customer's behalf",
  including "accessing websites, executing code, modifying files, sending
  communications".
- **§2.2.** The Customer authorises actions that "Grok Bot initiates
  autonomously" within the permissions the Customer or its Users grant.
- **§2.2.** The Customer is responsible for "monitoring recurring or
  unattended activities". So unattended running is expected.
- **§2.3.** "Customer will use appropriate human review before permitting
  Agentic Actions", including those involving external communications,
  financial transactions and data deletion. "Human" and "appropriate" are
  not defined.
- **§2.3.** Approval and automated-review controls "are provided as aids
  only".

The direction is one way. A human sits at the app; a non-human acts on
the far side of it. The terms require a human on the near side for
review, and nowhere describe a non-human taking that seat.

**The documentation shows non-human inputs the vendor does offer.** A
Bot can be started by a schedule, an event, another Bot, an email, or a
webhook called by a program. These are the "means" the Service itself
provides. A non-human system that starts a Bot through the webhook is
using a published feature. It cannot read the result there, answer an
approval, or do anything else the app does.

## 6. The three arrangements

### A. A human uses the app

Permitted. This is the product as sold.

### B. A non-human operates the app for an account holder

**Against, from the pages**

- The non-human access clause covers it in terms.
- The one doing the using is not "an individual".
- §2.3 calls for "human review" before the Bot is permitted to act. If
  the non-human system is the one granting approvals, no human is
  reviewing. An approval given by a non-human does not appear to be what
  that clause means.
- The eligibility rules cannot be met or failed by something with no age,
  which suggests such a user was never contemplated.

**For, from the pages**

- **The account holder is the user.** §3 of the Terms of Service makes
  the holder accept "responsibility for all activities that occur under
  your account". The policy contemplates that "you allow others to use
  the Service under your account". On this view the non-human system is a
  tool in the holder's hands, as a keyboard macro or a screen reader is.
- **The Customer can be a company.** A company acts only through others.
  The terms do not say those others must be employees.
- **The clause's heading.** The item sits under "Not complying with laws
  or regulations". Operating an app through software breaks no law I know
  of, so the item's placement suggests it targets fake accounts,
  scraping and abuse. The same list includes "creating fake accounts"
  beside it.
- **The vendor's own product does this elsewhere.** Grok Bot operates
  other companies' websites and apps for its users, by non-human means.

**Weighing them.** The "for" arguments give a fair account of who is
responsible. They do not remove the words "non-human means". A vendor
that wished to act against an account operated this way has a clause that
says so in terms, and a Cursor staff reply on a related matter shows a
strict reading: "Using your subscription outside official clients can
trigger abuse enforcement, up to and including an account ban." That
reply concerned unofficial clients, not a non-human operating the
official one.

**Position:** prohibited on the text; arguable on purpose; not permitted
anywhere.

### C. A non-human uses the app in its own right

**From the pages**

- It cannot make the age representation in §2.
- It cannot give registration "information about yourself" that is
  "accurate, complete, and not misleading" while presenting as a person.
- The policy prohibits "creating fake accounts" and "Misleading others or
  not being transparent regarding your use of AI".

**General law**

- A contract needs a party with legal capacity. An artificial
  intelligence, however capable, is not a legal person in any
  jurisdiction I know of. It cannot agree to terms, own an account, or be
  held to a promise.
- The law does recognise software that forms agreements for someone:
  an "electronic agent" binds the person who deploys it. That is
  arrangement B again. There is always a person or company behind the
  system, and that party is the one bound.

**Position:** not possible under these terms. An account opened this way
would belong to nobody who could hold it, and would be a fake account
within the policy.

## 7. Does capability change anything?

No, in either direction.

- **Nothing in the terms turns on how capable the system is.** A script
  that clicks one button and a system more capable than any person are
  both "non-human means".
- **Nothing in the terms rewards capability.** The eligibility rules ask
  for adulthood, not competence. A system that could act more carefully
  than any human reviewer still is not a "human" for §2.3 and still is
  not an "individual".
- **General law does not currently give standing to any artificial
  intelligence.** The question of whether it should is open in
  scholarship and unsettled as policy; it is not the law that these terms
  operate under today.

## 8. What would make it permitted

- **Written consent from the vendor** for a named account to be operated
  by a non-human system. This is the only thing that settles it.
- **Using the entrances the vendor provides for non-humans.** The routine
  webhook is one. It allows starting work and nothing more.
- **Keeping a human as the user.** If a person holds the account, grants
  the approvals, and performs the "human review" the terms require, with
  a non-human system assisting that person, the arrangement moves toward
  A. How much assistance turns the person into a bystander is not
  something the terms define.

## 9. What is not known

- **Whether "individual" excludes non-humans by intent.** The word is
  undefined, and the drafters may simply not have considered the case.
- **Whether the vendor enforces the non-human access clause against
  operation of its official app.** No statement or reported action was
  found.
- **The "Agreement" the Grok Bot Terms rest on**, where a company has
  one. An order form or master agreement could define users differently.
  The Terms of Service say use under a company's master services
  agreement "is governed by that MSA".
- **The x.ai legal pages**, which refuse automated reads (403).
- **The law of Texas**, which the Terms choose as governing (§16.1), and
  the law of other countries.
- **Any law made specifically about artificial intelligence as a user of
  online services.** None was read for this report.
